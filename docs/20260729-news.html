🏛️ Regulation & Policy

44 US State Attorneys General Challenge CFTC's Authority Over Sports Prediction Markets
A coalition of 44 state attorneys general sent a letter to the CFTC asserting the agency lacks authority to regulate sports prediction markets, urging it to withdraw proposed rules and create new ones compliant with the Commodity Exchange Act and Constitution. Sports betting has historically been regulated at the state level, and the attorneys general argue CFTC's proposed rules would dramatically expand federal oversight beyond statutory authorization. Courts across states remain divided, with Minnesota temporarily blocking enforcement while New York rejected halting action against Kalshi.

Hungary Repeals Crypto Transaction Validation Rule, CoinCash Secures First MiCA License
Hungary's parliament voted to repeal mandatory third-party validation for certain crypto conversions, removing requirements to verify asset origins, wallet ownership, and customer information before transactions. Separately, the National Bank of Hungary granted CoinCash operator Tiwala Solutions the country's first MiCA authorization on July 20, covering custody, crypto-to-fiat and crypto-to-crypto exchange, transfers, investment advice, and portfolio management. This dual development signals Hungary's pivot toward streamlined regulation while ensuring compliance with EU's MiCA framework.

Russia Charges Telegram Founder Pavel Durov with Aiding Terrorism, Issues Arrest Warrant
Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) formally accused Telegram founder Pavel Durov of aiding terrorist activities and issued an international arrest warrant. The FSB alleges the charges stem from Telegram's failure to remove content used by Ukrainian special services as well as terrorist and extremist organizations to prepare and coordinate sabotage, terrorist activities, and cyber fraud in Russia. Neither Durov nor Telegram had immediately commented on the allegations.

Russia's Central Bank Publishes Draft Regulations for Crypto Platforms and Custodians
Russia's Central Bank released draft regulatory rules for crypto exchanges, digital custody institutions, and token issuers. The draft stipulates that digital custody institutions must maintain minimum net capital between 50 million and 250 million rubles (approximately $600,000 to $3 million), with specific amounts varying by business type. These rules will supplement Russia's Digital Currency and Digital Rights Law, which passed this month and is expected to take effect September 1, 2026. Non-qualified investors will be limited to purchasing mainstream assets like BTC, ETH, and USDT with an annual cap of $4,000, while industry participants will have a transition period until March 1, 2027 to complete compliance and approval procedures.

Lawmakers are also considering abolishing the 22% crypto asset tax originally scheduled for 2027 implementation.
